A year ago, Agjenti AI was three files on my laptop and a conviction that
nobody asked for. Today it's a real product, with real users, and a list
of mistakes long enough to fill this entire article.

I'm writing this down for the same reason I write everything down here:
so the version of me five years from now can see exactly what seventeen
felt like, instead of remembering it the way I want to.

## The idea was never the hard part

Everyone assumes the hardest part of building an AI product is the AI.
It isn't. The hardest part is deciding, every single day, what *not* to
build. Agjenti AI could have been ten different products in its first
three months. It became one because I forced myself to say no to nine
of them.

> The version of your product that ships is never the most interesting
> one you thought of. It's the smallest one you could defend.

## What actually moved the needle

- Talking to the first ten users like they were the only ten that would
  ever exist.
- Shipping the unglamorous parts (billing, onboarding, error states)
  before touching another model integration.
- Writing in public, even when what I had to show was ugly.

## What I'd do differently

I spent too long optimizing a landing page nobody was reading yet, and
not nearly enough time in the actual product with actual users watching
me over a screen share. If there's one lesson from year one, it's that
conviction is not the same as evidence — and I mistook the first for the
second more than once.
